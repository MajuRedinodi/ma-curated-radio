# Replacing the Yamaha RX-V1900 — decision, 2026-09-22

Verified against manufacturer pages, live stock status and independent
reviews on 2026-09-22. Every "best of 2026" listicle was ignored: two of
them were caught stating specs that were flatly wrong (a sub out that
does not exist, a phono input that does not exist).

## Hard requirements

1. A **true phono stage** (MM at minimum; MC keeps a future cartridge
   upgrade unconstrained)
2. A **line-level subwoofer output** for the Klipsch R-120SWi (run wired
   over LFE)
3. **No loss of real power or headroom** against the RX-V1900's honest
   two-channel output (~100W)
4. **Bass management** — a high-pass on the R-60M bookshelves — because
   with bookshelves plus a sub this is a sound-quality feature, not a
   convenience
5. **In production and buyable today**, at the lowest cost that meets
   the above
6. Only two sources are used: WiiM Pro (RCA) and the turntable

## The pick

### Parasound NewClassic 200 Integrated — $1,000, in stock

| | |
|---|---|
| Power | **110W × 2, both channels driven, 20Hz–20kHz, 0.05% THD** (125W at 1%) |
| Phono | **MM (40dB / 47kΩ) and MC (50dB / 100Ω)** |
| Bass management | **Analogue, variable 20–140Hz high-pass on the mains**, plus an 80Hz low-passed sub out |
| Sub control | Level and on/off **on the remote** |
| Also | Burr-Brown PCM1798 DAC (optical/coax to 24/192), home-theatre bypass, headphone amp, tone controls |
| Availability | **Add to Cart live on parasound.com**, with a 30-day "Reserve" price lock |
| Price | **$1,000 direct** — the reviewed retail was $1,195, so this is already discounted |

Independent review (The Audio Beatnik) on the phono stage: "deep black
backgrounds and a refined purity in the tonality of instruments and
voices … care has gone into its design." On power: "ample power on tap"
into difficult 4Ω speakers. Negatives: would have liked better speaker
binding posts; headphone amp is "somewhat dry." Nothing on reliability.

Why it wins: it is the **only finalist buyable today**, it is **$300
cheaper** than the other, it meets every hard requirement with an honest
rating rather than a marketing one, and the sub level on the remote is
genuinely useful for a bookshelf-plus-sub room.

Connect the WiiM Pro by RCA into a line input, bypassing the amp's DAC.

## The alternate

### Emotiva BasX TA2+ — $1,299, temporarily unavailable

135W × 2 (both driven, 20Hz–20kHz, <0.02%), MM/MC phono, variable
40–200Hz high-pass bass management, XLR in/out, HDMI ARC, USB-C.
Shipping since March 2026; the live product page reads "Temporarily
unavailable" while listing 70 units, so a hold rather than a
discontinuation. Reviews are clean (SoundStage measurements, Absolute
Sound); one real note is that its **cooling fans become audible when
pushed hard**, and the remote and menu draw complaints.

Choose it over the Parasound only if it comes back in stock and you
want XLR or HDMI ARC. The extra 25W and the wider crossover range make no
audible difference into efficient Klipsch with a sub.

## If the budget will not stretch

The **Yamaha A-S501 (~$600)** meets the two stated must-haves — real MM
phono and a line-level sub out — at 85W. You give up bass management
and MC. It is not a compromise on anything you said you needed, only on
the thing that would make the bookshelves sound their best.

## Before buying anything

Test the sub with any spare RCA cable from a drawer. Everything that
happened on the night of 2026-09-22 — the swelling, the dropouts, every
apparent fix that did not hold, the final silence after the cable was
pulled and re-run — is consistent with one broken interconnect. If that
is what it was, this document is a plan for whenever the Yamaha actually
dies, not a purchase for this week.

## Cartridge note

The AT-LP120XBT-USB ships with an AT-VM95E, moving magnet. The best-value
upgrade is the **AT-VM95ML stylus (~$170)**: a stylus-only swap on the
same body, no realignment. Both finalists' phono stages handle any VM95
stylus and any MM or high-output MC cartridge you are likely to choose;
the Parasound and TA2+ also handle low-output MC if you ever go there.
